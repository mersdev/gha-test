Malaysia Day

What is it?

Malaysia Day commemorates 16 September 1963, the day Malaya, North Borneo (Sabah), Sarawak, and Singapore formally united to form the Federation of Malaysia. It marks the founding of the modern nation as it was originally conceived.

The Formation

The idea of merging the territories was proposed by Tunku Abdul Rahman in 1961. After negotiations and the Cobbold Commission's assessment of local support, the four territories formally merged on this date, creating a larger federation. Singapore later left the federation in 1965, but Sabah and Sarawak remain key parts of Malaysia today.

Why It Matters

Malaysia Day was only officially recognized as a public holiday nationwide in 2010, following calls — especially from Sabah and Sarawak — for equal recognition alongside Hari Merdeka, since Malaysia as a country didn't exist until 1963.

How It's Celebrated

  • National-level celebrations rotating among states
  • Cultural showcases from Sabah, Sarawak & Peninsular Malaysia
  • Flag-raising ceremonies and community events
  • Public holiday across the whole country
